# Product Review — CEO Recommendation

Date: August 14, 2026
Recommendation: **GO, with the revised narrow scope**

## Executive position

I would push us to do this, but I would push the revised version—not a generalized progress module or an expanding education feed.

The strongest product opportunity is to make the current engine legible at the moment a user can understand it. A saved observation should feel like it changed something. A confusing app mechanic should have one nearby explanation. Then the feature should get out of the way.

That creates a better product without touching the engine and without pretending Well Within can replace method instruction.

## What changed after review

The review materially tightened four things:

1. **Existing UI is locked.** The Calendar stays a complete month grid. Current summary, Catch Up, Today, Help, History, and Cycle cards are not redesign scope.
2. **Lessons are distinct from the summary.** The summary owns current chart interpretation; lessons own durable app mechanics and chart-reading skills.
3. **The library is finite.** Viewed/dismissed lessons do not recycle every cycle.
4. **Users control the surface.** They can dismiss one topic or hide contextual tips, while status and safety education remain available.

These changes make the feature more credible and reduce its largest downside: cluttering the screen with repetitive content.

## Why I would fund it

### 1. It improves the weakest part of a strong engine

The engine can be correct while the experience still feels inert. If users cannot see what a save changed, understand why similar-looking states differ, or know where to learn, engine quality does not fully translate into perceived value.

This work closes that translation gap.

### 2. It teaches at the moment of relevance

Help requires the user to know she has a question. Contextual education exposes one useful concept only after the corresponding product state exists. That should increase comprehension with less cognitive load than expanding onboarding.

### 3. It respects product maturity

A finite lesson system is scaffolding. Successful scaffolding eventually disappears. That is preferable to a permanent content card fighting the daily chart for attention.

### 4. It reinforces trust

The revised source policy is conservative:

- reviewed Well Within copy is the shipping source;
- official method material informs alignment and boundaries but is not copied;
- method-level additions require qualified review;
- no generative or remote tip content;
- the app remains brand-neutral and does not claim formal instruction.

That stance is consistent with a privacy-first, transparent charting product.

## Expected outcome

| Outcome | Expected direction | Confidence | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition that Save worked and changed the chart | Material improvement | High | The first-save acknowledgement closes a visible feedback gap |
| Understanding of Calendar treatments and recorded-versus-empty states | Material improvement | High | The first lessons point directly to UI the user just created |
| Understanding of summary versus general education | Improvement | Medium/high | Explicit topic ownership and duplicate suppression protect the distinction |
| Second/third distinct-day charting | Moderate improvement | Medium | More perceived value and clarity should give some users a reason to return |
| Seven-day retention | Small-to-moderate improvement | Medium/low | Clarity helps, but routine fit, motivation, reminders, and life context remain larger forces |
| First completed chart | Small improvement | Medium/low | Acknowledgement helps persistence, but this feature does not solve daily habit formation alone |
| Trust / reduced pressure | Mild improvement | Medium | Finite content, user control, and no streaks reduce the risk of nagging |
| Acquisition or immediate revenue | Little direct change | High | This is activation/retention infrastructure, not a distribution or monetization feature |

I would not present a retention lift as a promise. Directional targets for a later authorized measurement plan could be:

- 10–20% relative improvement in reaching a second distinct observed date;
- 5–10% relative improvement in seven-day return;
- no increase in required entry taps;
- no increase in users mistaking the app for ovulation confirmation or formal method instruction.

Those are investment thresholds, not current evidence.

## The education topics I would prioritize

Version one should teach only app literacy with existing approved foundations:

1. how an observation appears on the Calendar;
2. why Dry, Not observed, and no entry are different;
3. how sensation and appearance work together;
4. what the app does with multiple observations in one day;
5. what can be reviewed in one completed chart.

Likely confusion areas for later research include Peak-type versus Peak Day, P+ markers, bleeding/mucus layering, incomplete versus Not observed, and unsupported special contexts. Those topics are important, but they should not be added merely because they are methodically complex.

The correct sequence is:

1. observe direct Well Within user confusion;
2. identify the decision or mistake the lesson should prevent;
3. draft against approved sources;
4. obtain the required practitioner/clinical review;
5. release only if it is not duplicative of the current summary-to-Help path.

## What happens when the user sees everything

The lesson surface goes away.

I do not recommend recycling the same lessons at the start of every cycle. The upside is low and the likely result is habituation: users stop seeing the card, and the app begins to feel like it is filling space.

Instead:

- archive lessons in Understanding Your Chart for deliberate review;
- preserve viewed/dismissed state across cycles;
- allow Hide chart tips and a reversible re-enable control;
- resurface revised content only through an explicit product decision when the new version has new value.

This is better product discipline than trying to keep the surface alive forever.

## Principal risks

### Risk 1 — Calendar crowding

The Calendar is already vertically dense. The mockups therefore preserve the full grid and place one compact row below Today in the natural scroll flow. If native QA still feels crowded, the correct fallback is to omit the contextual row—not compress or redesign the Calendar.

### Risk 2 — Summary duplication

Duplicate messages make both surfaces feel less trustworthy. Topic IDs, explanation-target mappings, and a copy-level duplicate review are core functional requirements.

### Risk 3 — Method instruction by accident

Official materials emphasize trained instruction and follow-up. Product lessons must explain Well Within, not claim to teach the full system. Any method-level explanation needs a named qualified reviewer and a source/version record.

### Risk 4 — Lesson system becomes a CMS

Do not build remote content, editorial scheduling, recommendation services, or a large taxonomy. A typed local catalog of roughly five lessons is enough to test the hypothesis.

### Risk 5 — Novelty wears off

It will. That is acceptable. The goal is early comprehension and confidence, not permanent engagement with the lesson surface.

## Go/no-go gates

Before implementation:

- approve the locked existing-UI rule;
- approve the five-topic version-one catalog;
- confirm source/reviewer ownership;
- confirm that local preference storage is acceptable;
- do not authorize new method-level copy by implication.

Before release, five-user usability review should show:

- 5 of 5 recognize that the full Calendar is unchanged;
- at least 4 of 5 can explain what the lesson teaches without repeating the summary;
- at least 4 of 5 can tell Dry from no entry after the first lesson;
- at least 4 of 5 can find the dismiss/hide controls;
- at least 4 of 5 understand that hidden tips remain in Help;
- 0 of 5 interpret lesson completion as fertility progress or method certification.

If users cannot distinguish the lesson from the summary, revise topic/copy once. If the distinction still fails, ship only the first-save acknowledgement and completed-chart acknowledgement.

## Final CEO call

**Do it, but make it small enough to earn the right to expand.**

The revised feature has a credible chance to improve comprehension and early return behavior because it explains the value already created by the engine. It will not transform acquisition, and it should not become a content product. Its strategic value is making a trustworthy engine feel understandable, useful, and human while preserving the product users already have.
