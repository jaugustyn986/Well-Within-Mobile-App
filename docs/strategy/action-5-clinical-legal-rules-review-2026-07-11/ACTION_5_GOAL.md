# Action 5 Goal Contract

/goal Clinically and legally de-risk Well Within's rules engine and user-facing fertility interpretation, then design a progressive-disclosure UX based only on validated outputs.

Verification: inventory every engine rule and every Peak, fertile-window, TTC, confidence, method, diagnostic-adjacent, privacy, and accuracy claim across the app, Help, onboarding, PDF export, App Store copy, and social; produce separate rule-provenance, claim-review, correctness, clinical-fixture, source, and UX-surfacing artifacts; run the rules-engine test suite; identify specification contradictions and calendar-date defects; map representative fixtures to required clinical review.

Constraints: do not invent Creighton rules, present Well Within as official or certified, copy proprietary training materials or sticker artwork, add diagnosis or treatment claims, provide avoiding-pregnancy instructions, or treat automated tests as clinical validation.

Boundaries: the first pass is read-only outside this Action 5 documentation folder. Do not change production rules, user-facing clinical copy, App Store metadata, public content, production data, authentication, sync, or deployment state until the relevant clinical, legal, and product decisions are approved.

Iteration policy: complete rule provenance first, then claim inventory and correctness review, then UX recommendations. Use authoritative sources and qualified-review questions; make at most three focused reconciliation passes, and use a new source of evidence after repeated uncertainty.

Stop when: every existing rule and claim family has a source/status/owner, correctness defects and unsupported patterns are explicit, the clinical reviewer has a bounded fixture pack, and each major app surface has a proposed information hierarchy with acceptance criteria.

Pause if: a proprietary source or license is required, a clinical interpretation lacks qualified approval, or trademark, copyright, medical-device, advertising, privacy, or jurisdiction-specific legal judgment is needed.
