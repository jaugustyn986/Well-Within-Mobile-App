## Prior-Week Audience Intelligence

The July 15-20 listening work used bounded public Instagram metadata, TTC/FAM community discussions, and app-store/product reviews. No authenticated Instagram or TikTok comment inspection was available.

The evidence sharpened four related needs:

1. Tracking must fit real routines. Travel, poor sleep, busy months, and competing signs can make charting feel burdensome or obsessive.
2. Users want a detailed, flexible record without surrendering interpretation to the app.
3. Atypical or edited entries must remain dependable and readable; privacy alone does not compensate for lost, rigid, or unreliable records.
4. Restraint is part of trust. Users distinguish keeping observations from trying to force certainty out of them.

Representative language included “doesn’t become obsessive and it stays productive,” “it didn’t feel like a burden because it fit my lifestyle,” “my chart was thrown all out of whack,” “the app doesn’t make predictions,” “as detailed as you’d like,” “no way to exclude an anomalous cycle,” and “it gets messy fast.”

Strategic implication:

- The listening evidence continues to support H3's user job: calm, user-owned capture that remains readable on imperfect days.
- It also strengthens a future H2 listening question about when tracking stops feeling useful and starts feeling like pressure.
- These are qualitative, medically sensitive problem-language signals. They do not prove Instagram performance, target-audience prevalence, product reliability, or any fertility outcome.
