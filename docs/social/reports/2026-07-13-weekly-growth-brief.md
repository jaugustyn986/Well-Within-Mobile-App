## Prior-Week Audience Intelligence

The July 7-13 listening work used public privacy discussions, fertility-awareness educator material, app reviews/listings, and TTC/FAM community threads. It did not use authenticated Instagram or TikTok comments.

The evidence converged on five related needs:

1. Privacy must come with utility. People want local control, but they also need reminders, history, portability, and a record that paper will not lose.
2. People want observation capture without an app overriding their judgment. They describe definitions that do not click, subjective signs, and discomfort with prediction-heavy tools.
3. "Easy" cannot mean shallow. Users want quick daily entry alongside enough detail for irregular cycles, multiple signs, and personal factors.
4. Readability matters. People want relevant observations visible together instead of split across views or forced into a prescribed structure.
5. The emotional cost is cognitive load. Disrupted sleep, device changes, inconsistent routines, and conflicting inputs can make charting feel like personal failure or cause spiraling.

Useful language captured this week includes "scared about my privacy," "record observations in your own words," "straightforward but detailed enough," "visually see everything in one place," "a looot of brain power," "chart was thrown all out of whack," and "it didn't feel like a burden because it fit my lifestyle."

Strategic implication:

- The Week 1 audience-language sprint now appears to have met its quantity threshold of at least ten exact observations and has produced multiple sharper directions.
- The best direction is resilient capture plus readable history for imperfect routines, without prediction or interpretation claims.
- This is qualitative problem-language evidence. It is not medical guidance, verified product proof, or evidence that a specific Instagram hook/format will perform.
